# #0025 INCENTIVE HOLE IN THE GATE, raised by Dharmin on 2026-08-26 and he is right: three of the five gate checks are configured by the party they are meant to constrain. His words: 'wouldn't the merchant always give a very high amount as exposure limit so as to increase their revenue, that doesn't make sense. shouldn't the buyer agent be the one to have this spending limit'. The policies table is keyed on merchant_id, so the merchant sets spend_cap, approval_threshold and category_allowlist. A merchant's incentive is to sell more, so a rational merchant sets the cap as high as it will go — meaning checks 2, 3 and 4 are self-imposed by the seller and constrain nothing. Only check 1, the authoritative re-read, is genuinely adversarial to the merchant, because it catches the merchant's own catalog contradicting its own checkout claim. A Razorpay panel would find this in one question, and it undercuts the central pitch that this is 'the underwriting layer that makes self-serve safe'. Underwriting a counterparty you cannot vouch for cannot use limits that counterparty chose. THREE POSSIBLE OWNERS, and the fix is mostly framing rather than code. (a) BUYER-OWNED: the agent's human sets the budget and allowlist, which is what AP2 signed mandates and UPI Reserve Pay actually do. Real constraint, but it changes the pitch from merchant underwriting to buyer-side agent budget control, and it needs a migration since policies is PK'd on merchant_id. (b) PLATFORM-OWNED: Razorpay assigns the limits per merchant by trust tier — an unvetted merchant may receive at most X per agent per window until it builds a track record. This preserves the original thesis exactly, fixes the incentive problem, and needs NO schema change: the same table, different owner and different narrative. (c) BOTH, effective limit = min(buyer budget, platform ceiling), which is how real risk systems work. RECOMMENDATION under a 5 September deadline: take (b) as the framing now and record (a) as the honest next step. Evidence that (b) is the natural reading: in the live claude.ai demo the model itself described the allowlist as 'the categories this shopping agent is allowed to buy from' — it read the policy as a constraint ON THE AGENT, not a merchant sales preference. So the reframe matches how a reader already interprets it. ACTIONS: DUK-21 must state who owns the policy and why; DUK-20's onboarding UI copy must not present the caps as merchant-chosen sales settings; and the limitations list needs the per-agent policy named as future work. Related to #0024, which is the buyer-funding half of the same asymmetry.
